What Does an HAGB Decision Mean? Conditions, Supervision Period and Consequences After the 2026 Amendment

10 September 2026

One of the most frequently issued yet least understood decisions in Turkish criminal proceedings is the deferral of the announcement of the verdict, known as HAGB. Defendants are often told that it means "you got off clean". In fact HAGB is not an acquittal; it is the suspension of an established conviction for five years. A setback during the supervision period may lead to the suspended verdict being announced and the sentence being enforced.

The HAGB institution was comprehensively reorganised in 2026. In this article we examine the current conditions, how the supervision period works, the effects of the decision on criminal records and public employment, and the legal remedy available against it.

1. What does an HAGB decision mean?

At the end of the trial the court concludes that the defendant committed the offence and determines a sentence, but does not announce the verdict. Under Article 231 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, HAGB means that the verdict produces no legal consequences for the defendant, except for provisions on confiscation. If the defendant completes the supervision period in compliance with the rules, the verdict is set aside and the case is dismissed.

HAGB is therefore a decision in which the first-instance court accepts that the defendant is guilty. For a defendant who believes they could be acquitted, HAGB may be not an achieved result but a judgment that should be challenged.

2. The 2026 amendment

Law No. 7589 of 16 July 2026 completely rewrote the paragraphs of Article 231 concerning HAGB, and the amendment entered into force on 31 July 2026. The new provision regulates the conditions, the supervision period, the consequences of breaches during that period and the legal remedy in greater detail. Some of the information found in sources published before that date is no longer valid.

3. Conditions for an HAGB decision

Under the new text, the court may issue an HAGB decision if the following conditions are met together:

  1. The sentence imposed is imprisonment of two years or less, or a judicial fine,
  2. The defendant has not previously been convicted of an intentional offence,
  3. Taking into account the defendant's personality and conduct during the trial, the court is convinced that they will not reoffend,
  4. The damage suffered by the victim or the public as a result of the offence has been fully remedied by restitution, restoration or compensation.

For a defendant unable to remedy the damage immediately, an HAGB decision may also be issued on condition that the damage is paid in full in monthly instalments during the supervision period. Even where the conditions are met, the court is not obliged to grant HAGB; the law gives it discretion. HAGB cannot be applied at all to torture and ill-treatment offences or to offences committed by public officials in the course of their duties that amount to ill-treatment.

A notable feature of the new provision is that a prison sentence in a verdict subject to HAGB can no longer be suspended, nor converted into alternative sanctions even if it is short. The court must choose between these three institutions.

4. The five-year supervision period

A defendant who receives an HAGB decision is placed under supervision for five years. For a part of this period not exceeding one year, the court may impose obligations as a probation measure, such as attending a training programme, working under supervision, or being barred from or required to attend certain places. The limitation period for prosecution does not run during the supervision period.

An important restriction introduced by the new text is that no second HAGB decision may be issued for an intentional offence committed during the supervision period. In other words, HAGB is not available twice within the same supervision period.

5. What if an offence is committed during supervision?

If the defendant intentionally commits a new offence or breaches the obligations imposed during the supervision period, the court announces the suspended verdict. The new provision gives the court some flexibility here: assessing the situation of a defendant who failed to comply with the obligations, the court may establish a new verdict by deciding that up to half of the sentence will not be enforced, or, where the conditions are met, that the prison sentence will be suspended or converted into alternative sanctions. The announced or newly established verdict may be challenged by objection.

Points to watch are whether the new offence must have ended in a final conviction before the verdict can be announced, and whether the date of the new offence falls within the supervision period. These questions form the basis of any objection against an announced verdict.

6. If the supervision period is completed successfully

If no new intentional offence is committed during the supervision period and the obligations are complied with, the suspended verdict is set aside and the case is dismissed. HAGB decisions are recorded in a special system separate from the criminal record. These records can be viewed only when requested by a public prosecutor, judge or court in connection with an investigation or prosecution. An HAGB decision therefore, as a rule, does not appear on a criminal record certificate.

7. Is HAGB an obstacle to public employment?

Article 48 of the Civil Servants Law requires that applicants have "not been convicted" of certain offences. Since an HAGB decision does not produce the consequences of a conviction, it does not, as a rule, defeat this requirement. However, in positions requiring security clearance and archive checks, in fields governed by special professional laws such as law enforcement and the judiciary, and in certain licensing processes, HAGB records are assessed differently. The outcome depends on the type of offence and the special legislation of the institution concerned; giving a general answer would be misleading.

8. Legal remedy against an HAGB decision

Since the amendment made by Law No. 7499 in 2024, HAGB decisions are challenged by appeal (istinaf) rather than objection. The 2026 provision retains this system. The regional court of appeal examines the decision for procedural and substantive unlawfulness, and the general provisions on appeal to the Court of Cassation apply to the regional court's decisions.

Whether to accept an HAGB decision is a strategic choice. If the evidence makes acquittal likely, it may be better to appeal rather than accept a five-year supervision period. This decision requires the evidence in the file, the court's reasoning and the defendant's personal circumstances to be assessed together.

9. Conclusion

Used correctly, HAGB is a valuable institution that gives the defendant a second chance; but treating it as an acquittal leads to serious misconceptions. The 2026 amendment has reshaped its conditions and consequences. Whether to accept the decision, how to manage the supervision period and how to challenge an announced verdict all require a case-specific assessment.
