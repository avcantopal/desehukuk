Work Permits for Employers Hiring Staff from Abroad: 7 Points That Decide the Application

16 July 2026

From shipyards to construction, from textiles to tourism, qualified foreign staff are no longer a preference but a necessity in many sectors. Yet legally bringing an employee who is abroad to Türkiye is a process with far more stages than most employers expect. A small mistake in this process can turn weeks of waiting into a rejection and leave the employee unable to come to the country at all.

Below we summarise the seven critical points that, in practice, determine the fate of applications.

1. The process is not a single application

The most common misconception among employers is that a work permit is a single application made in Türkiye through e-Devlet. For a foreign national who is abroad, however, the process runs in two different countries, before two different authorities, and the two stages depend on each other. The application in Türkiye cannot be made until the stage begun at the Turkish consulate in the foreign national's country is completed; if the application in Türkiye is incomplete, the steps taken at the consulate are wasted.

Following the two stages in the right order, with the right documents and in parallel, determines whether the process is completed in a month or takes several months.

2. Every country behaves differently at the consulate stage

The documents required for the foreign employee's application to the Turkish embassy or consulate in their own country can vary from one consulate to another. Each consulate has its own practice regarding the scope of the medical report, the issue dates of documents, which employer documents must be submitted and the appointment system. An employee who goes to the consulate with an incomplete or incorrect file may have to start the same process all over again.

3. Wage and employment criteria shape the application from the start

In assessing a work permit, the Ministry requires that the wage paid to the foreign national is not below the minimum wage multiples set according to the nature of the position, and that the workplace employs a certain number of Turkish citizens relative to the number of foreigners. These criteria vary according to the position, the sector and the situation of the workplace.

If the wage in the employment contract or the number of employees at the workplace does not meet these criteria, this alone is enough for the application to be rejected, even if every other element is complete. The contract should therefore be prepared according to these criteria before the application.

4. The question "Why will you employ a foreigner?"

During the application, the employer is asked to explain why it needs foreign staff for the position in question. This question is not a formality. The justification must be consistent with the workplace's field of activity, the expertise required by the position and the state of the domestic labour market. A carelessly written justification becomes the weakest link in the assessment.

5. Who applies in a subcontractor structure?

Where the foreign employee will work at the main employer's workplace but within a subcontractor, it is particularly important through which company the application is made and how the relationship between the two companies is shown to the Ministry. In sectors where subcontracting is common, such as shipyards and construction, an incorrectly structured arrangement can lead to rejection of the application and later to disputes over liability for employment receivables.

6. Deadlines continue to run after the permit is issued

Approval of the work permit is not the end of the process. The law sets short and strict deadlines for paying fees and card charges, visa procedures, and the social security and address notifications that must be made after the employee enters Türkiye. A work permit is usually granted for a limited period on the first application; filing the extension application on time also requires separate follow-up.

Missing any of these deadlines can expose the employer to administrative sanctions at the end of an otherwise well-managed process.

7. The cost of employment without a permit is high

International Labour Force Law No. 6735 imposes an administrative fine on employers who employ foreign nationals without a work permit, separately for each foreign national, and doubles the fine if the act is repeated. While a foreign national found working without a permit is reported for deportation, the employer must cover the accommodation, return travel and, where necessary, medical expenses of the foreign national and any family members.

In other words, the approach of "let them start working until the permit comes through" is not merely a risk of a fine for the employer; it is the risk of losing entirely the staff in whom it has invested.

In short

  • The process runs in two countries, before two separate authorities, and the stages depend on each other.
  • The wage and the employment structure at the workplace must be brought into line with the criteria before applying.
  • Documents, the justification and the contract must be prepared specifically for each file.
  • The notification deadlines after the permit is issued are short and carry sanctions.

Setting up the process correctly from the start

The work permit process requires a file-specific assessment at every stage, from drafting the employment contract to the consulate appointment, from the Ministry application to the notifications after the employee enters Türkiye. This is also why two applications made from the same country for the same position can have different outcomes.

If you plan to bring staff from abroad, clarifying the legal framework of the process before the contract is signed and the consulate appointment is booked will save you both time and cost.
