Deportation Orders and Administrative Detention in Turkey: What to Know So You Do Not Miss the Seven-Day Deadline

25 June 2026

For a foreign national living in Türkiye, a deportation decision often begins with an unexpected identity check on the street. Working without a work permit, overstaying a visa or residence permit, or facing a criminal allegation can lead to the person being taken to a removal centre within a short time. At this stage the deadlines provided by law are extremely short, and once missed they usually cannot be remedied.

In this article we examine, in light of the amendments made in 2024, the circumstances in which a deportation decision is issued, the deadline for challenging it in court, the remedy against administrative detention, and the persons who cannot be deported.

1. Who issues the decision?

Under Article 53 of Law No. 6458 on Foreigners and International Protection, a deportation decision is taken by the governorships, either on the instruction of the Presidency of Migration Management or of their own motion. The decision, together with its reasons, is notified to the foreign national, their legal representative or their lawyer. If the foreign national is not represented by a lawyer, they must be informed of the outcome of the decision and of the procedures and deadlines for challenging it.

2. In which circumstances is deportation ordered?

Article 54 of the Law lists the grounds for deportation. The most common in practice are:

  • Being found to be working without a work permit,
  • Overstaying a visa or visa exemption period by more than ten days, or having a visa cancelled,
  • Having a residence permit cancelled, or overstaying it by more than ten days without an acceptable reason,
  • Being assessed as a threat to public order, public security or public health,
  • Using false information or forged documents in entry, visa or residence procedures,
  • Violating the rules on legal entry into or exit from Türkiye.

The "public order" ground is often used in practice without being based on any concrete fact. Questioning which fact the decision relies on, and whether that fact really constitutes a threat, forms the basis of the lawsuit.

3. Persons who cannot be deported

Article 55 provides that certain persons cannot be deported even if they fall within Article 54. These include persons for whom there are serious indications that they would face the death penalty, torture or inhuman treatment in the country of return; those for whom travel is risky due to serious health problems, age or pregnancy; those undergoing treatment for a life-threatening illness for which no treatment is available in the country of return; victims of human trafficking receiving victim support; and victims of violence until their treatment is completed. This assessment must be made individually for each person.

4. The deadline for filing a case: seven days

Under Article 53(3), as amended by Law No. 7533 of 21.11.2024, the foreign national, their legal representative or lawyer may challenge the deportation decision before the administrative court within seven days of notification. The claimant must also inform the authority that issued the decision that a case has been filed.

The court decides within fifteen days of the file being completed, and its decision is final. A result must therefore be obtained with a single lawsuit; preparing the petition and evidence completely the first time is decisive.

The most important safeguard in the Law is that, unless the foreign national consents, they cannot be deported during the period for filing a case or until the proceedings are concluded. A case filed within seven days is the most effective tool for ensuring that the person remains in the country. Once the deadline is missed, this protection disappears.

5. Administrative detention and removal centres

The governorship may order administrative detention for persons subject to a deportation decision who pose a risk of absconding or disappearing, who have violated entry or exit rules, who have used forged documents, who have not left within the period granted, or who are considered a threat to public order. These persons are taken to a removal centre within forty-eight hours.

Administrative detention may not exceed six months; where the foreign national does not cooperate, it may be extended by up to a further six months. The governorship must assess every month whether continued detention is necessary. An application against the administrative detention decision may be made to the criminal judgeship of peace, which must conclude its examination within five days. The application does not suspend detention, but a new application may be made on the ground that the conditions have changed.

6. Alternatives to administrative detention

Article 57/A provides for alternative obligations that may be applied instead of administrative detention: residing at a specified address, reporting regularly, depositing a guarantee, electronic monitoring and family-based return are the main ones. The total duration of these obligations may not exceed twenty-four months. A stable address, a regular employment relationship or family ties in Türkiye are strong grounds for requesting these measures instead of detention.

7. When ordinary remedies are exhausted

Since the administrative court's decision is final, where there is a serious risk to the right to life or of ill-treatment, an individual application may be lodged with the Constitutional Court, together with a request for an interim measure to stop the deportation. This application must also be made within thirty days of the final decision.

8. For employers

Employing a foreign national without a work permit is a ground for deportation for the employee and also leads to heavy administrative fines for the employer. Businesses employing foreign staff protect both the employee and the company by setting up their processes correctly from the outset. See our article on work permits for foreign nationals.

9. Conclusion

Time is the most valuable factor in the deportation process. The seven-day period for filing a case is the critical threshold that determines whether the person stays in Türkiye, and the remedy against administrative detention is the key to the release of a person held in a removal centre. Properly presenting the reasoning of the decision, the person's family and social ties and the risks in the country of return directly affects the outcome.
