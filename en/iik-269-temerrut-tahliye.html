Eviction for Default Under Article 269 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law: Validity Requirements of the Notice

14 January 2026

The fastest route available to a landlord for evicting a tenant who does not pay rent is enforcement proceedings without judgment with a request for eviction. The speed of this route, however, comes from its strict formal requirements. A defect in the payment order, a miscalculated deadline or an irregularity in service can end months of proceedings without an eviction order.

1. Legal framework

Under Article 269 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, where the proceedings concern ordinary or usufructuary leases and the creditor so requests, the payment order includes the notice set out in Article 315 of the Turkish Code of Obligations and the warning that, after the statutory period has expired, the debtor's eviction from the leased property may be requested from the enforcement court.

Article 315 provides that if the tenant fails to pay rent or ancillary costs that have fallen due after delivery of the property, the landlord may give the tenant a period in writing and notify that the contract will be terminated if payment is not made within that period. This period is at least ten days, and at least thirty days for residential and covered commercial leases, and begins on the day following the written notification to the tenant.

Under Article 269/a, if the debtor does not object and does not pay the rent within the notice period, the enforcement court orders eviction at the creditor's request made within six months of the end of the notice period.

2. Preconditions for eviction for default

According to the case law of the Court of Cassation, the following conditions must be met together for eviction for default to be requested from the enforcement court:

  1. The subject of the proceedings must be a rent receivable.
  2. Eviction must be expressly requested in the enforcement request, together with attachment.
  3. A payment order of form no. 13, requesting attachment and eviction, must be served on the tenant.
  4. The payment order must correctly state the thirty-day payment period for residential and covered commercial premises.
  5. The rent that is the subject of the proceedings must not have been paid within this period, and eviction must be requested from the enforcement court within six months of the end of the period.

The enforcement court cannot order eviction unless all of these conditions are met.

3. The order in which the enforcement court examines the request

According to the case law, the enforcement court examines the eviction request in a certain order:

  • Enforcement request: first, whether the creditor's enforcement request complies with the law. In particular, if eviction was not requested in the enforcement request, the eviction request is rejected.
  • Payment order: next, the lawfulness of the payment order is examined. If the payment (notice) period is stated incorrectly or incompletely, is not stated at all, or the payment order contains no eviction warning, in other words if the document served is not a form no. 13 payment order, the eviction request is rejected.
  • Objection: if the enforcement request and payment order are lawful, the court examines whether the debtor objected to the payment order in time.
  • Payment: finally, the court examines whether the debtor paid the rent within the notice period.

For the tenant's lawyer, the defence should start from the first steps of this order. The most common grounds for rejection in practice are an incorrectly stated period in the payment order and the absence of an express eviction request in the enforcement request.

4. The objection period and finality of the lease relationship

Upon service of the payment order, the debtor must notify the enforcement office of the grounds of objection within seven days. If the debtor does not clearly and definitively deny the lease agreement and, where applicable, their signature on it in the objection, they are deemed to have accepted the agreement.

According to the case law, if the debtor does not expressly deny their signature on the agreement and objects only to jurisdiction, the debt and accrued interest, the lease relationship is deemed established. In that case the creditor, relying on one of the documents listed in Article 68, may request from the enforcement court both the removal of the objection and eviction (Article 269/c).

If the debtor does not object in time, the proceedings become final. This does not mean, however, that the creditor can request eviction immediately; the debtor may pay the rent within the thirty-day payment period. According to the case law, if the debtor pays the rent within the payment period following service of the payment order, the eviction proceedings without judgment come to an end and the creditor cannot request eviction.

5. When deadlines begin: the requirement of proper service

The objection, payment and default periods provided for in Article 269 begin to run only upon proper service of the payment order on the debtor. If the payment order is returned unserved, the periods do not begin, and the creditor cannot request removal of the objection or eviction for default from the enforcement court.

If service is alleged to be irregular, the date of knowledge determined following a complaint of irregular service is taken as the basis. According to the case law, where the date of knowledge is taken as the basis, an eviction case filed before the thirty-day period has expired is rejected. The first thing the tenant's lawyer should check is therefore the certificate and date of service; the landlord's lawyer, in turn, should make sure that the period has actually expired before requesting eviction.

6. Is non-payment of ancillary costs a ground for eviction?

This question, long debated in practice, has been settled in the landlord's favour by recent case law of the Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation. The dispute concerned whether the enforcement court can order eviction where the rent was paid within the thirty-day period but ancillary costs subject to the proceedings, such as common charges, electricity and water, were not paid.

According to the case law, Article 315 provides that the tenant's default not only in paying rent but also in paying ancillary costs is a ground for termination, and the legal consequence of non-payment of rent or of ancillary costs is the same. If the lease agreement provides that ancillary costs are to be paid by the tenant and the debtor did not object to the payment order within the statutory period, the ancillary cost debt and its amount become final. In that case, since the ancillary costs were not paid within the thirty-day period, default must be deemed to have occurred and the tenant's eviction must be ordered.

In practice this means: if the tenant did not object in time to ancillary costs included in the payment order, having paid only the rent does not protect them from eviction.

It should be noted that this conclusion was adopted by majority. According to the dissenting view, Article 269/a expressly refers to "rent debt", and ancillary costs are not included in the concept of rent debt. Non-payment of ancillary costs can only be raised in an eviction case filed before the general courts. This view remains important for the tenant's defence in cases where there is no agreement in the contract on the amount of ancillary costs.

7. Partial payment and allocation of payments

Whether a payment made within the payment period covers the entire debt subject to the proceedings determines the fate of the eviction request. Partial payment does not remove default. For the creditor, issues such as which items money deposited into the enforcement file without explanation will be allocated to, and whether the rent remains unpaid after deduction of the collection fee, must be raised in the eviction request. For the tenant, making the payment in a way that covers all the items subject to the proceedings and before the payment period expires is the only safe way to avoid eviction.

8. Checklist for landlords and tenants

Landlord's (creditor's) lawyer

  • Check that eviction is expressly requested in the enforcement request together with attachment.
  • Make sure the payment order is form no. 13 and the thirty-day period is correctly stated.
  • Add ancillary costs to the proceedings item by item on the basis of the contract.
  • Verify the date of service; do not request eviction before the period has expired.
  • Do not miss the six-month period for requesting eviction after the period ends.

Tenant's (debtor's) lawyer

  • Examine the certificate of service; if there is an irregularity, file a complaint.
  • Check that the period and eviction warning in the payment order are complete.
  • Object to the ancillary cost items and amounts within the seven-day objection period.
  • Pay the entire debt subject to the proceedings within the thirty-day period, specifying the items.

9. Conclusion

Eviction under Article 269 is a route strictly bound by form that delivers quick results. For the landlord, the key to success is drafting the enforcement request and payment order completely and tracking the deadlines correctly. For the tenant, the first seven days are decisive: failing to object within this period makes the entire debt subject to the proceedings, including ancillary costs, final, and unless full payment is made within thirty days, eviction becomes inevitable.
