Uncontested Divorce in Turkey: Conditions for a Single-Hearing Divorce and Critical Mistakes in the Protocol

3 March 2026

For spouses who have decided to end their marriage, uncontested divorce is usually the fastest route, often concluded in a single hearing. The speed of the process, however, depends on the care with which the divorce protocol is prepared. Every vague expression on custody, maintenance, division of property or jewellery opens the door to new lawsuits that may continue for years after the divorce.

In this article we examine the legal conditions for uncontested divorce under Turkish law, the headings that must be included in the protocol, the most common mistakes in practice, and the effects of the 2024 legislative amendment.

1. Legal conditions

Under Article 166(3) of the Turkish Civil Code, an uncontested divorce may be granted only if the following conditions are met:

  1. The marriage has lasted at least one year,
  2. The spouses apply to the court together, or one spouse accepts the case filed by the other,
  3. The judge hears the parties in person and is satisfied that they have expressed their will freely,
  4. The judge approves the arrangement accepted by the parties regarding the financial consequences of the divorce and the situation of the children.

The judge may make any changes to the protocol deemed necessary in the interests of the parties and the children. If the parties also accept these changes, the divorce is granted. The protocol must therefore be balanced and enforceable enough to pass the judge's approval.

2. Headings that must be included in the protocol

A well-prepared divorce protocol should resolve in advance every issue that could give rise to disputes after the divorce. The main headings are:

  • Custody of the children and the contact arrangements with the non-custodial parent: weekends, religious holidays, mid-term and summer holidays,
  • The amount, payment date and annual increase of child maintenance,
  • Whether spousal maintenance is provided for either spouse,
  • Claims for pecuniary and non-pecuniary compensation and any waiver of them,
  • Who keeps the jewellery,
  • Liquidation of the matrimonial property regime, including real estate, vehicles, bank accounts and debts,
  • Arrangements on litigation costs and attorneys' fees.

3. The most common mistakes in practice

Most problems in uncontested divorce files stem from protocols prepared hastily from ready-made templates. Common mistakes include:

  • Stating the maintenance amount without specifying how it will increase; in that case a separate lawsuit is needed for every increase.
  • Leaving the division of property open by stating that "it will be discussed separately later", which turns into years of matrimonial property litigation after the divorce.
  • Drafting contact arrangements in unenforceable terms such as "at times agreed by the parties".
  • One party signing a general waiver without fully understanding which rights they are giving up.
  • Failing to address the possibility that the custodial parent may move abroad with the child.

A protocol shows its real value not when it is signed but when a problem arises after the divorce. A provision not drafted clearly enough to be enforced directly by the enforcement office remains on paper only.

4. How does the process work?

The case is opened with a petition submitted to the family court together with the protocol. The court usually sets a hearing date within a short time. At the hearing both spouses must be present in person and declare before the judge that they wish to divorce and accept the protocol. If the judge approves the protocol, the divorce is granted at the same hearing.

The parties waiving their right of appeal considerably shortens the time needed for the decision to become final. The final decision is entered in the civil registry and the spouses' marital status changes. If one of the spouses lives abroad, the process must be planned separately because of the requirement to be heard in person.

5. If the marriage has not yet lasted a year

Uncontested divorce is not available before the one-year period has passed. In that case the spouse seeking divorce must file a contested divorce case on the ground that the marriage has irretrievably broken down and prove that claim. On the other hand, an ongoing contested divorce case can be converted into an uncontested divorce once the marriage has passed the one-year mark and the parties agree on a protocol. This is one of the most effective ways of bringing long contested cases to a quick conclusion.

6. The 2024 amendment: waiting period after a dismissed case

Law No. 7532 amended the fourth paragraph of Article 166 of the Civil Code. Under the new provision, if a divorce case filed on any ground is dismissed and the spouses fail to resume their common life within one year of that decision becoming final, the marriage is deemed to have irretrievably broken down and divorce is granted at the request of either spouse. Under the previous provision this period was three years. The amendment has opened a new route for spouses whose divorce case was dismissed.

7. Conclusion

When prepared correctly, uncontested divorce is the least damaging route for both the spouses and the children. The brevity of the process, however, does not mean the protocol can be simple. Drafting the provisions on maintenance, custody, contact and division of property clearly, fairly and enforceably is the most important safeguard against new disputes after the divorce.
