How Can a Tenant Be Evicted in Turkey? Personal Need, Undertakings, the Ten-Year Rule and Other Legal Routes

12 May 2026

One of the questions landlords ask most often is in which circumstances and how a tenant can be evicted. In residential and covered commercial leases, Turkish law strongly protects the tenant; the expiry of the lease term is not in itself a ground for eviction. The Turkish Code of Obligations does, however, allow the landlord to terminate the lease when certain conditions are met. What these options have in common is that they are tied to short and strict deadlines.

In this article we examine the main eviction routes, including personal need, the needs of a new owner, a written undertaking to vacate, two justified notices, the ten-year extension period and non-payment of rent, as well as the mandatory mediation process.

1. Basic rule: expiry of the term is not a ground for eviction

Under Article 347 of the Code of Obligations, in fixed-term residential and covered commercial leases the contract is deemed extended for one year on the same terms unless the tenant gives notice at least fifteen days before the end of the term. The landlord cannot terminate the contract merely because the term has ended. One of the grounds listed in the law is required for eviction.

2. Need of the landlord or their relatives

Under Article 350, the landlord may file an eviction case if they need to use the property as a residence or workplace for themselves, their spouse, descendants, ascendants or other persons they are legally obliged to support. In fixed-term leases, the case must be filed within one month of the end of the term.

The need must be genuine, sincere and compelling. As emphasised in the decision of the 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation dated 01.03.2018 (Docket 2017/8802, Decision 2018/1953), temporary needs, and needs that have not yet arisen or depend on a long period to materialise, cannot be grounds for eviction; the need must exist on the date the case is filed and continue throughout the proceedings. According to the same decision, the existence of the need is assessed together with all the evidence, including site inspection and expert examination.

3. Need of a new owner

Under Article 351, a new owner who has purchased the leased property may terminate the contract through a case filed six months after acquisition, provided that the tenant is notified in writing within one month of the acquisition. The new owner may also choose to wait until the end of the lease term. Once the one-month notification period is missed, this defect cannot be cured.

4. Reconstruction and major renovation

If the property needs to be rebuilt, or substantially renovated, extended or altered for reconstruction purposes, and it cannot be used during these works, the landlord may file an eviction case. Properties within the scope of urban transformation are subject to the special provisions of Law No. 6306.

5. Written undertaking to vacate

Under Article 352(1), if the tenant, after the property has been delivered, undertook in writing to vacate it on a certain date but fails to do so, the landlord may terminate the contract by applying to the enforcement office or filing a case within one month of that date.

The validity of the undertaking is one of the most disputed issues in practice. Undertakings signed together with the lease or before the property is delivered carry the risk of being deemed invalid on the ground that they do not reflect the tenant's free will. Allegations that blank dates were later filled in are also a frequent subject of objection.

6. Two justified notices and owning a home in the same district

If, within one lease year, the tenant caused two justified written notices to be served for non-payment of rent, the landlord may file a case within one month of the end of that lease year. This route is an effective tool against tenants who consistently pay late.

If the tenant or their cohabiting spouse owns a habitable residence within the boundaries of the same district or municipality and the landlord was unaware of this when the lease was concluded, a case may also be filed within one month of the end of the contract.

7. The ten-year extension period

Under Article 347, in fixed-term leases, at the end of the ten-year extension period the landlord may terminate the contract without giving any reason, provided that notice is given at least three months before the end of each subsequent extension year. The ten-year period is calculated not from the start of the contract but from the end of the initial lease term. In indefinite-term leases, the landlord may terminate by notice under the general provisions once ten years have passed since the start of the lease.

8. Non-payment of rent

If the tenant fails to pay the rent, the landlord may terminate the contract by sending a formal notice under Article 315 or initiate enforcement proceedings without judgment with a request for eviction. Eviction through enforcement, although strictly bound by formal requirements, is one of the fastest routes. For details, see our article on eviction for default under Article 269 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law.

9. Mandatory mediation before filing a case

Since 1 September 2023, applying to a mediator before filing a case has been a precondition in disputes arising from lease relationships. Eviction cases fall within this scope; however, requests for eviction through enforcement proceedings are not subject to the mediation requirement. Agreeing on a specific eviction date at mediation and recording it in a proper agreement makes it possible to obtain a result without lengthy proceedings. The mediation application must also be taken into account when calculating the deadlines for filing a case.

10. Conclusion

Evicting a tenant is one of the areas where being right is not enough. Each ground for eviction has its own notice and filing deadlines; if one of these deadlines is missed, the contract is extended for another year and the landlord has to wait for the next period. Which route to follow should be planned from the outset according to the type of contract, its start date and the documents available.
