"I Rented Out My Account and Was Summoned to Testify": The Criminal Consequences of Renting a Bank Account in Turkey

20 August 2026

Social media posts offering "extra income from home", "lend us your account for a few days and keep a commission" or "help us process cargo payments" usually end the same way: a few weeks later the phone rings and the account holder is summoned to the prosecutor's office to give a statement about a victim they have never heard of. A person who saw themselves as a mere intermediary, or even as a victim, finds that they are listed in the file as a suspect of aggravated fraud.

In this article we examine the criminal-law position of people who rent out their bank account, card, online banking credentials or crypto account, the new provision that entered into force in 2026, and the points that shape the course of the investigation from its very first day.

1. Why is account renting so widespread?

In fraud committed by phone, through fake listings or over social media, the perpetrator's main problem is to make the money taken from the victim untraceable. A fraudster using an account opened in their own name is identified quickly. Organised groups therefore first route the money into third parties' accounts and from there quickly disperse it to other accounts or crypto platforms.

The account holders who form the first link in this chain are usually young people, people in financial difficulty or job seekers. In return for a few thousand lira they hand over their cards, passwords or mobile banking access. The real owner of the money often leaves no trace at all, and the only real person named in the investigation is the account holder.

2. What offence is the account holder charged with?

When a victim is deceived into sending money to a bank account, this constitutes aggravated fraud committed by using banks, credit institutions or information systems as an instrument, under Article 158(1)(f) of the Turkish Criminal Code. For this subparagraph the minimum prison sentence is four years and the maximum ten years, and the judicial fine may not be less than twice the benefit obtained from the offence. If the offence is committed jointly by three or more persons the penalty is increased by half, and if it is committed within the activities of a criminal organisation it is doubled.

Even if the account holder did not personally carry out the fraudulent act, they are tried as a participant in the offence. In practice, whether the account holder is treated as a joint perpetrator or as an accessory depends on whether they withdrew the money themselves, who gave the transfer instructions and their role in planning the act. This distinction directly affects the sentence.

3. The 2026 amendment: the reduction introduced by Article 158(4)

Law No. 7589, adopted on 16 July 2026 and published in the Official Gazette on 31 July 2026, added a new fourth paragraph to Article 158 of the Criminal Code. Under this provision, where participation in simple or aggravated fraud is limited to handing over to another person, in order to obtain an unjust benefit for oneself or for someone else, payment instruments such as one's own or another person's bank or credit cards, or the essential information or tools that enable an account to be used, the penalty is reduced by half.

The provision covers not only banks but also brokerage firms, payment service providers and crypto-asset service providers. The amendment has two important consequences:

  • Account renting has not been decriminalised. The reduction affects only the amount of the penalty, not the existence of the offence.
  • The reduction is intended for persons whose contribution is limited to handing over the account or the credentials. A person who withdrew the money, transferred it to other accounts or contacted the victim may not benefit from it.

The transitional provision of the Law also affects judgments delivered before the amendment. For defendants whose case is pending on appeal and who may benefit from the new paragraph, the judgment is quashed and the file is returned to the first-instance court. Convicts whose judgment has become final and is being enforced may benefit from the effective repentance provisions if they fully compensate the victim's loss within six months of a warning issued by the court.

4. Is every account holder guilty? The question of intent

Criminal liability requires that the account holder knew that their account would be used for fraud, or at least foresaw this possibility and accepted it. A person who shared their details believing they were applying for a genuine job, who was deceived into a seemingly legitimate transaction such as a salary or cargo payment, or whose card was used without their knowledge is in a different position from someone who knowingly rented out their account for a fee.

At this point courts look less at the account holder's statement and more at the concrete facts in the file. When and for what purpose the account was opened, to whom and how the cards and passwords were handed over, by whom and how quickly the money arriving in the account was withdrawn, whether the account holder obtained any benefit, and the messages exchanged between the parties are decisive in establishing intent. The same incident may end in acquittal or in a heavy conviction depending on how these facts are presented.

5. Effective repentance, HAGB and reducing the sentence

In fraud offences, compensating the victim's loss is one of the factors that most affects the sentence. Under Article 168 of the Criminal Code, if the loss is fully compensated by restitution or payment before prosecution begins, the penalty may be reduced by up to two thirds; if it is compensated after prosecution begins but before judgment, it may be reduced by up to one half.

With the new Article 158(4) reduction, this calculation has changed significantly. The four-year minimum for aggravated fraud falls to two years when halved for a person whose contribution was limited to handing over the account; once discretionary mitigation and effective repentance are also applied, the resulting sentence may fall below two years. This opens the door to institutions such as deferral of the announcement of the verdict (HAGB) or suspension of the sentence. These outcomes are not automatic, however; the timing and manner of compensating the loss must be planned together with the structure of the defence.

Although simple fraud falls within the scope of mediation in criminal matters, aggravated fraud committed through a bank or information system does not. The legal consequences of any contact with the victim should therefore be assessed in advance.

6. If you have been summoned to give a statement

In such files the direction of the investigation is often set by the first statement. Statements given in panic, later changed, or contradicting the bank records in the file may be used as evidence of intent. The suspect's right to consult a lawyer before giving a statement and the right to remain silent are guaranteed by the Code of Criminal Procedure.

  • Do not delete messages with the person who rented your account, screenshots of the listing or payment records.
  • If you notice activity on your account, apply to your bank and keep proof of your application.
  • Do not contact the victim or the persons using the account without first consulting your lawyer.
  • Before going to the prosecutor's office or the police, find out under which offence the file is being conducted.

7. Beyond the criminal case: civil liability

The account holder's liability is not limited to the criminal file. Victims who sent money to the account may bring claims for damages and restitution against the account holder on the basis of tort or unjust enrichment. Banks may freeze accounts because of suspicious transactions and end the banking relationship by classifying the person as a high-risk customer. In files with several victims, the total of these claims far exceeds the "rent" received.

8. Conclusion

For the account holder, renting out an account may look like easy money; in the eyes of the legal order it is participation in a heavily punished fraud offence. Although the 2026 amendment introduced a significant reduction for persons whose contribution was limited to handing over the account, it did not remove the offence itself. When the existence of intent, the limits of the contribution and the strategy for compensating the loss are set up correctly at the outset of the investigation, the outcome of the file can change entirely.
