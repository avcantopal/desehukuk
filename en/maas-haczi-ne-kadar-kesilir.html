Wage Garnishment in Turkey: How Much of a Salary Can Be Deducted, and Can a Pension Be Attached?

27 March 2026

The greatest concern of a debtor facing enforcement proceedings is whether their salary can be attached and how much of it will be deducted. For the creditor, wage garnishment is often the only regular source from which the debt can be collected. Turkish law strikes a balance between these two interests: it limits the attachable portion of salaries and leaves certain income almost entirely outside the scope of attachment.

In this article we examine the statutory limits of wage garnishment, the order applied where there are several attachments, the special protection for retirement pensions, and the remedies available against wrongly applied attachments.

1. Legal basis: the one-quarter rule

Under Article 83 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, salaries, all types of wages and similar income may be attached after deducting the amount the enforcement officer considers necessary for the subsistence of the debtor and their family; however, the amount attached may not be less than one quarter of such income. Article 35 of the Labour Law expressly provides that no more than one quarter of an employee's monthly wage may be attached, assigned or transferred.

When these two provisions are applied together, wage garnishments are in practice generally made at one quarter of the net salary. Debtors earning the minimum wage are not excluded from this rule; one quarter of the minimum wage may also be attached.

2. A different rule for maintenance claims

After setting the one-quarter limit, Article 35 of the Labour Law reserves the rights of maintenance creditors. For attachments made for maintenance claims, the one-quarter limit therefore does not apply in the same way; the enforcement officer may set a higher deduction rate, taking into account the essential needs of the debtor and the persons they support. Attachments for maintenance claims also do not wait their turn behind other attachments.

3. If there are several attachments

If several creditors have attached the same salary, the attachments are ranked. Under Article 83, deductions for a later attachment cannot begin until the deductions for the earlier attachment have been completed. In other words, several one-quarter deductions are not made from the debtor's salary at the same time; the next creditor is paid only after the first creditor's claim has been paid in full.

This rule also explains why it is important for the creditor to notify the employer of the attachment as soon as possible. A place in the queue can mean collection starting months or even years earlier or later.

4. Can a retirement pension be attached?

Under Article 93 of Social Security Law No. 5510, the income, pensions and allowances of insured persons and their beneficiaries cannot be attached except for the Social Security Institution's own receivables and maintenance debts. Unless the debtor consents, requests to attach such income are rejected by the enforcement officer.

The most common problem in practice concerns general consent clauses obtained when signing a bank loan or credit card agreement. Under Article 83/a of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, agreements made in advance that non-attachable assets and rights may be attached are not valid. Consent given when signing a contract is therefore not sufficient for a retirement pension to be attached; the consent must be given at the time of the attachment.

5. Attachment of the bank account into which salary is paid

Creditors often attach the debtor's bank accounts instead of sending a garnishment order directly to the employer. Where an attachment is placed on the account into which a salary or pension is paid, the nature of the money in the account is important. The protection against attachment granted to retirement pensions is not lost simply because the pension has been paid into a bank account. For an employed debtor's salary account, it must also be assessed whether the one-quarter limit has been exceeded.

6. Complaint against a wrongful attachment

Where income that cannot be attached has been attached, the one-quarter limit has been exceeded or the ranking rule has not been observed, the debtor may file a complaint with the enforcement court within seven days of learning of the action, under Article 16 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law. To the extent that non-attachability relates to public order, however, it may also be raised without being bound by a time limit. Which situations are subject to a deadline varies according to the type of attachment and the debtor's circumstances.

7. What can be done before wage garnishment

Wage garnishment usually follows once the enforcement proceedings have become final. In enforcement proceedings without judgment, an objection to the payment order within seven days stops the proceedings and can prevent the salary from being attached. If the existence of the debt is not disputed, a payment plan agreed with the creditor or an undertaking given at the enforcement office makes it possible to manage both the amount and the duration of the deductions.

For the creditor, sending a garnishment order to the employer identified from the debtor's social security records, and pursuing the legal remedies against an employer that fails to respond to the order or to make deductions, are the steps that determine the speed of collection.

8. Conclusion

The general rule in wage garnishment is one quarter; however, maintenance claims, retirement pensions and cases involving several attachments lead to very different outcomes under this rule. For the debtor, asserting non-attachability rights in time, and for the creditor, tracking the ranking of attachments and the employer's obligations, are the main factors that determine the outcome.
